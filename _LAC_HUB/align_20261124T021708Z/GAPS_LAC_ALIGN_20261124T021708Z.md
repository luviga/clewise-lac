## Judgement calls and their reasons

### Published targets (step 2)

- **Long-term document after 2035.** The LTS scenario follows a long-term document after 2035 only where the document states a value that covers years after 2035. Costa Rica's plan states a renewable matrix from 2030 to 2050 (CR-T090), so LTS takes 1.0 from 2036. Guatemala's long-term document states 64 percent by 2032 (GUA-T060), below the NDC's 80 percent for 2035, so the NDC value is held: an earlier, lower milestone does not lower the later commitment. Changing least.
- **Plans cited by the NDC.** Where the NDC in force states no value for a year and a plan the NDC cites does (Dominican capacity in the NDC itself; Ecuador PME 2023-2032 capacity; Jamaica IRP 2022 capacity for 2041), the plan value loads in the NDC scenario. Where the NDC states a value for the same year, the NDC wins (Honduras 0.70 over the plan's 0.76 and 0.71; Jamaica 0.63 over the IRP's 0.634).
- **Capacity rule inputs.** The anchor-year generation of the renewable plants is the Stage 0 recommended share of the anchor year times the Stage 0 generation of that year; the capacity factor of a plant kind is the anchor-year CF x AF weighted by the anchor-year generation of the plants of that kind (bypass plants left out); the denominator is the Stage 0 generation of the scenario in the target year. The IRP 2022 capacity for Jamaica counts additions from 2022, so the 2022-2024 additions are counted twice (in the anchor generation and in the added capacity); the share is slightly high.
- **Share above 1.** Ecuador's PME 2023-2032 adds 4,034 MW of hydro and 1,683 MW of other renewables by 2032; with the model's own capacity factors this produces more than the model's whole 2032 generation (rule value 1.35). The rule writes at most `max_target_share` = 1.0. Decision for Luis.
- **LTS below NDC after 2035 in Ecuador.** The LTS curve runs from the NDC value of 2035 (1.0, held from 2032) to PLANMICC's 0.89 in 2050, so Ecuador's LTS renewable share after 2035 is below the NDC's held 1.0.
- **Ecuador 2050 share.** PLANMICC states two shares of SNI generation for 2050 (non-conventional renewables 0.35, hydro 0.54); their sum 0.89 is the renewable share loaded, against the model's national generation (the model does not separate SNI and isolated systems).
- **Renewable share definition.** Stage 1 counts the hydro and non-hydro renewable classes of the power plants (name tokens of `technology_types.csv`) over all power plant generation. Each country's definition (`share_definitions.csv` of the NDC verify job) is national generation with the renewable list of its document; the model counts bagasse and biogas as renewable in every country and has no own use, so model generation is read as net (Jamaica). Imports are not generation in either.
- **Transport targets.** Written only where the model's adoption rows map a published fleet share to one class or a group of classes: Costa Rica and Honduras. Honduras' rows have milestones 2030, 2040 and 2050 only, so the 2035 value is written in the 2040 column and held; the 2035 share interpolates between 3 and 7.5 percent. Ecuador's policy scenarios have no adoption rows; Guatemala's targets are vehicle counts and a Transmetro share; Jamaica's LTS fleet targets fall before 2035 where the NDC (which states no fleet share) rules. These are listed in `published_values_loaded.csv` with their reasons.
- **Stylised transport rows.** Adoption rows of the policy scenarios that carry no published value (for example electric shares 0.1, 0.3, 0.3 in Guatemala and the Dominican Republic, and the JAM_TRANSPORT NDC rows) stay as they were and appear in `scenario_difference_register.csv` as policy measures without a published source.
- **Emission caps (step 2.7).** Label `CO2e_sources` (emissions of the fuels supplied to the energy system) carries the Honduras and Dominican LTS caps, one label per country. Neither NDC 3.0 states a 2050 value (`work/lts_2050_checks.csv`); r is an ASSUMPTION with a range.
- **Locked plants after the release.** With every upper limit open from 2036, the Dominican LTS solve moved all fossil generation to the biomass plant in one year (PPBIM 1.1 PJ in 2035, 114 PJ in 2036, 235 PJ in 2050; energy emissions 36.1 Mt in 2035 and 10.5 Mt in 2036, far under the cap of 35.3 Mt): the model holds no biomass supply limit, and at zero emission penalties biomass power is the cheapest plant. Stage 1 holds the locked plants (non-hydro renewables other than solar and wind: biomass, biogas, bagasse, geothermal) at their BAU share in every policy scenario, so the release keeps their controller upper limit; solar, wind, hydro and fossil plants stay open. Decision for Luis (the brief says upper limits open).
- **Honduras r = 0.37 (step 2.8).** With r = 0.47 the Honduras LTS solve has no feasible solution. Relaxing one limit group at a time on the capped data file: activity lower limits, activity upper limits, minimum capacity and the power plant capacity caps each leave it infeasible; dropping the capacity caps of the electric vehicle classes alone makes it OPTIMAL. Those caps come from the published fleet targets (HON-T026 to T029, Tech_Adoption rows of LTS), and the model's emissions are counted at fuel supply (diesel, fuel oil, LPG, gasoline grow to 2050), so the cap falls on road transport and industry that the fleet targets hold. r in 0.005 steps: 0.37 OPTIMAL, 0.375 no feasible solution. r = 0.37 is loaded as ASSUMPTION (the low end of the stated range). Decision for Luis.

### Rules of the pipeline (steps 3 to 6)

- **One rule step for the solver data files.** Hydrogen at zero and history identity are written by a pipeline step (`scenario_rules.py`) on the B1 data files before Stage 0, so every Stage 0 and Stage 3 solve carries them. The B1 solves themselves and `B1_Output_Params` of the policy scenarios keep the scenario workbook history rows; the gate `history_identity.csv` is read on the Stage 3 data files.
- **Stage 2 overflow.** Frozen output (hydro, bagasse, geothermal and the other locked renewables) is kept; solar and wind keep their boundary-year output plus committed builds; the fossil plants give way down to their committed builds (minimum capacity investment). The existing output of a fossil plant with a committed build is not committed.
- **Frozen output source.** The frozen value of each plant is its share in the last override year (the research-corrected history of `_STAGE2_INPUTS`) times the Stage 0 generation of the boundary year; `frozen_output.csv` lists each plant with the override note.
- **Open bounds in the history gate.** In the Dominican data files 119 history cells of TotalTechnologyAnnualActivityUpperLimit read 99999 in BAU (Stage 3 writes a missing BAU row with that value) and 99999999999 in NDC and LTS (the block default the history rule copies). Both are no limit; `history_identity.py` counts them apart and reports 0 differences.
- **History identity by rule.** The brief names the Dominican (65 cells) and Jamaica (6 cells) history rows; the rule equalises every history value of every model, which also covers the other history differences of the compiled inputs (demand, maximum capacity, output ratios).
- **Imports.** Guatemala's import capacity is the Mexico-SER limit (240 MW, EOR 2021) plus one SIEPAC import limit (300 MW; the Guatemala simultaneous export limit equals each single limit, so two SIEPAC limits are not added). Honduras: 280 MW from the north plus 140 MW from the south. Guatemala's 2018 and 2024 import values keep their compiled values (ASSUMPTION); CNEE records cover 2019 to 2023. The Honduras history values (2018-2024) keep their compiled values; ODS and ENEE import records were not searched.
- **Emission factors.** Dominican and Honduran BAU factors equal IPCC 2006 Table 2.2 x the plant's fuel input within 6 percent (`work/ear_ipcc_check.csv`), so the policy rows take the BAU row. Ecuador's BAU factors differ by -28 to +57 percent, so Ecuador's plant factors take IPCC 2006 x fuel input with the AR5 GWP of Ecuador's inventory (p.50) in every scenario. The refinery row (PROCRU) is not a combustion factor and takes the BAU row. National inventory factors were not compared plant by plant.

### Costs (step 7)

- **Discount rates** come from secondary documents that tabulate the official rates (UNA thesis 2024 for Costa Rica, the Dominican Republic, Guatemala, Honduras; CEPAL Review for Ecuador). The official guides were not retrieved (MEPyD 2022 blocked, MIDEPLAN 2019 not online, DGIP 2015 404). Guatemala: the source says SEGEPLAN moved from 12 to 7 percent in 2022, while the IMF PIMA 2024 reportedly says no rate is set; 7 percent is used. None of the sources says real or nominal. Jamaica publishes none: mean of the five (10.262 percent).
- **Vehicle register** (subagent; `work/value_register_vehicles.csv`): a price cites a document only when it lies within 25 percent of a figure in it; otherwise an ASSUMPTION states the distance. Costa Rica FixedCost has no double division and is unchanged. Guatemala TRAUTHG and TRAUTPHG take the #18 ratios, which were computed against #18's own diesel car price, so their compiled prices (8,430 and 7,155 US$) sit below the import averages. Minibuses are not treated as buses for the range. The Guatemala Unit.Introduced label of TRAUTHG and TRAUTPHG changes from relative to TRAUTELE to relative to TRAUTDSL.
- **Penalties.** Every EmissionsPenalty of the energy and sector models is 0, except Ecuador ECU_AGR (unchanged by the brief) and Guatemala GUA_AFOLU, whose penalties are crop and livestock incomes kept in its own parameter files (Known-limitation candidate). External costs are reported for fuels of the energy models from the IMF template; waste and land-use external costs are not computed in `external_costs.csv`.
- **Liquid fuel conversion** of the IMF per-litre factors uses typical energy contents (gasoline 34.2, diesel 38.6, LPG 25.3 MJ per litre), an ASSUMPTION stated in the script.

- **Distances not written.** The vehicle register found sourced distances for the Dominican public and tourism buses (44,000 km, GEN-104), Guatemala heavy freight (9,000 km), Honduras tourism buses (44,000 km) and Jamaica cars and SUVs (20,000 km, JAM-002). Written, they made the Guatemala policy scenarios infeasible: the A2 compilers derive each fleet group's transport demand from fleet x Ref.km.BY, so a new distance rescales the calibrated activity and fuel use of every year (Guatemala heavy freight demand fell from 97 to 17 in the compiled units) while policy lower limits stay. All four changes are reverted; the source values stay in the register as the range and in Decisions for Luis.
- **Dominican policy plant rows.** The NDC and LTS rows of `B1_Scenario_Config.xlsx` (Electrical) carried plant caps and lower limits from an earlier recalibration to the stylised targets; with the common history they made the B1 policy solves infeasible. They take the BAU values (or the open cap 9999 where BAU has none) in every year: Stage 3 sets plant limits from 2025 from the published targets, so these rows were not policy measures of a published document.
- **Costa Rica policy plant rows.** The same holds in Costa Rica: the NDC and LTS plant caps from 2025 were about half the BAU caps (PWRGEO 0.267 against 0.525 GW in 2025). With the common history caps (the recorded capacities of step 5.1) the Stage 0 policy solves had no feasible path; tests on the solved data file showed the history caps of PWRFOI, PWRGEO, PWRSPVTRN and PWRWNONS bind together with these rows. They take the BAU values in every year (`work/register_policy_history_cr.csv`, 40 cells; two PWRHYDROR rows written on the first of two rows of the scenario, the one the runner reads).

### Jamaica transport labels (step 8)

- The model held an old `LTS` scenario (the national decarbonization plan, built on the A2 policy base labelled NDP) beside NDCNEW and LTSNEW. The old one is renamed NDP and stays active; NDCNEW and LTSNEW become NDC and LTS.
- The B1 runner keys every scenario by its `Reference` folder of `A2_Output_Params`. The A2 compiler wrote the policy base to `LTS` only, and NDCNEW, LTSNEW and LTSOPT read tracked copies of it (byte-equal at the start of this job, so they would have gone stale with this job's input changes). Pointing NDP, NDC and LTS at one folder made the runner apply the LTS adoption rows three times and the NDP and NDC rows never (it stopped with an index error). Each scenario now names its own folder (`Reference` NDP, NDC, LTS, LTSOPT) and the A2 compiler writes the policy base to the folder of every activated policy scenario of the Scenarios sheet; the 11 parameter files the compiler does not write are the same tracked files the removed folders held. All five scenarios OPTIMAL; the results creator runs.
