# Honduras: what a deeper study would still need

**Where the evidence stands.** The NDC in force is NDC 3.0 (HON-01, January 2026), checked against the second update (HON-02), which is a different document. Its values are all conditional on finance. The energy rows are a renewable share of 70 percent in 2030 held to 2035, a rise of 3 and 5 points in the electricity share of final use, 5 percent biofuels in transport, an electric fleet of 3 and 7.5 percent, and hybrid shares; the PIEG 2026-2035 (HON-08), which the NDC cites (p.26), adds capacity and share values. Of 192 values checked, 171 reached 3 of 3 (the 15-year BAU table by sector is held together by its own sums), 20 reached 2 of 3 and 1 is a conflict. No long-term strategy is published: `scenario_values.csv` has one row that says so. NDC 3.0 credits Climate Lead Group-Libelula with its drafting (HON-01 p.4), and the ENDRCH model documentation (HON-22) is CLG's.

**What is missing.**
1. The ENDRCH itself, when published, to replace the CLG documentation as the source of long-term values (67 percent below Tendencial in 2050 for the modelled sectors, HON-22 p.8, is a lead only).
2. The NDC 3.0 technical annex (measure sheets), announced but not published, which would define each indicator and its base year.
3. The SEN LEAP study behind the 2021 energy target (ENME p.9), and whether the NDC 3.0 BAU (a linear projection of the 2011-2020 inventory, HON-01 p.23) matches any model run.
4. A definition of the ENME fleet share (all vehicles or light vehicles) and vehicle stock data to compare with the model's transport shares; the NDC's 7.5 percent for 2035 is not in the ENME, which states 3 percent in 2030 and 15 percent in 2050 (HON-21 p.24).
5. The electricity share of final use in 2025 under the NDC's own accounting, to turn the 3 and 5 point rises into levels.
6. The PIEG 2026-2035 expansion (solar 1,796 MW, gas 1,285 MW and storage 215 MW by 2035) set against the model's installed capacity by year, which needs the plan's start capacities.
