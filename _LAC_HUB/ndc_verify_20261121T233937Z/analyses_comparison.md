**Costa Rica.** The ICE PEG 2022-2040 (CR-22, official, cited by NDC 3.0 for the 95 percent target) is a least-cost power expansion whose recommended plan keeps fuel oil at 17 GWh of 13,814 GWh in 2030 and 5 of 15,703 GWh in 2040 (p.202): its renewable share is near one, above the model's NDCNEW 0.897 to 0.900 and close to LTSNEW from 2035. The RAND and IDB study (CR-23, external) compares Without decarbonization (net 18.8 Mt CO2e in 2050) with the National Decarbonization Plan (net zero in 2050, net benefit 41 billion USD, p.49 to p.50); its NDP scenario is the reference for LTSNEW, its no-plan scenario is closer to the model BAU in direction (transport emissions grow), and nothing in it corresponds to NDCNEW, which the model builds as a 0.90 share with demand changes. Climate Action Tracker (CR-25 to CR-27) rates the 2023 targets and has not assessed NDC 3.0.

**Dominican Republic.** The World Bank CCDR (DR-21, DR-22, external) runs an electricity planning model with NP (current policies), NDC2030 and NZP2050: grid emissions of 14.3, 8.7 and 0.0 Mt CO2e in 2050 and 67 percent renewables in 2050 under NZP2050. NP is the nearest relative of the model BAU (renewables 0.339 in 2050) and NDC2030 sits between BAU and NDCNEW (0.750); NZP2050 is the counterpart of LTSNEW (0.990), which goes further on renewables than the World Bank's 67 percent. The CNE bulletin on the PEN 2025-2038 (DR-26, official) gives demand scenarios for 2038 but no renewable path. The CLG studies (DR-23, DR-24, lead) are the closest in method to the model (OSeMOSYS-RD, Tendencial against Estrategia, 76 percent below Tendencial in 2050 for all sectors) and should not be used as independent checks.

**Ecuador.** The World Bank CCDR (ECU-22) and IDB note 3000 (ECU-23) use the ELENA model, independent of the PLANMICC's OSeMOSYS model, with BAU, NDCu, NDCc and NZ (CCDR) or Base, APS, SDS and NZE (IDB). Both count large hydro as renewable: the CCDR BAU reaches 99 percent renewable power in 2050, above the model BAU (0.814) and NDCNEW (0.814) and equal to LTSNEW (0.990); its NZ scenario has 2.6 Mt CO2e from energy and transport in 2050 (p.52), far below LTSNEW's 30.4 Mt label. Their NDC scenarios follow the first NDC (assumptions to 2025), so none corresponds to NDCNEW, which is built on the second NDC's horizon. Climate Analytics (ECU-24) gives a 1.5 C path of 13 Mt CO2e in 2050 excluding LULUCF, for the whole economy.

**Guatemala.** No analysis found prints Guatemala scenario results that could stand beside the model. The ENDBE portal copy (GUA-21) is the same strategy as GUA-04. The CLG work on the ENDBE update (GUA-22, GUA-24, lead) defines high, medium and low scenarios in OSeMOSYS with no published results; it is the closest relative of LTSNEW. IRENA's Central America roadmap (GUA-26) prints only regional values (59 percent renewables in PES 2050, 97 percent in DES 2050), which bracket NDCNEW (0.750) and LTSNEW (0.990) but are not Guatemala values. NDC 3.0 itself (80 percent renewables by 2035) is above the model's NDCNEW 0.750.

**Honduras.** The SEN Prospectiva (HON-23, official) compares four scenarios against a reference (Escenario I): high electric vehicles, renewable power, and both, with CO2 from transformation and transport 19, 40 and 56 percent below the reference in 2050 (p.53). Escenario I is the nearest relative of the model BAU; Escenario IV is closer in kind to LTSNEW, whose energy label is 60 percent below BAU in 2050. The World Bank CCDR (HON-25) gives carbon-price scenarios with fossil CO2 cuts of 3, 9 and 20 percent in 2030 against the 2021 NDC BAU, comparable in size to NDCNEW's 7.6 percent cut of the energy label in 2030. The UNEP green economy study (HON-24, external) and the CLG ENDRCH documentation (HON-22, lead: 67 percent below Tendencial in 2050, renewables 70 to 75 percent) both build on the unpublished ENDRCH; LTSNEW's 0.990 renewable share is above the 70 to 75 percent of the ENDRCH documentation.

**Jamaica.** The World Bank and Vivid Economics study (JAM-21, external) is the analysis behind the 2020 NDC update: LEAP with Baseline, Unconditional NDC and Conditional NDC, cuts of 25.4 and 28.5 percent below BAU in 2030. Its baseline (8.2 Mt CO2e in 2030) sits above the model BAU energy label (5,951 kt in 2030) partly because it includes more sectors. Neither of its NDC scenarios belongs to NDC 3.0, so neither corresponds to NDCNEW. The jam-lts cost-benefit site (JAM-23 to JAM-28, lead, preliminary) defines Reference (2018 IRP) and LTS (fully renewable power by 2050, 540 MW of batteries by 2038, 67 percent electric fleets in 2050); its LTS is close in kind to LTSNEW (0.990 renewables in 2050), and its Reference follows the 2018 IRP while NDC 3.0 cites the 2022 IRP. Climate Analytics (JAM-22) supported the drafting of the LTS and prints no scenario values of its own.
