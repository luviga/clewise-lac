# Guatemala: what a deeper study would still need

**Where the evidence stands.** The NDC in force is NDC 3.0 (GUA-01, 2026): 23.7 percent below BAU in 2035 for net emissions, a BAU of 75,738.0 and a target level of 57,763.7 kt CO2 eq, which satisfy each other, and an energy and transport cut of 1,184.9 kt from 1,000 MW of renewables and E10. Its energy rows are 80 percent renewable generation by 2035 (p.40, p.102; the model has 0.750 in NDCNEW 2035) and at least 1,000 MW of additional renewable capacity. The long-term strategy is the ENDBE (GUA-04, FAOLEX copy; the UNFCCC file, GUA-21, was fetched in this job and has the same figures, though it is a 2021 reprint and not byte-identical). Of 126 values checked, 82 reached 3 of 3, 42 reached 2 of 3 and 2 are conflicts (one in the ENDBE, whose 125 Mt in 2050 does not fit its 22 percent label). NDC 3.0 credits CLG and two universities for technical support (p.5), and the ENDBE update under way is CLG work (GUA-22, GUA-24).

**What is missing.**
1. The MARN 2026b Climate Promise report (the BAU extrapolation and measure sheets behind NDC 3.0) and the UVG LULUCF tool, both cited by GUA-01 but not public.
2. The MEM Plan de Expansion Indicativo de la Generacion 2024-2054 (site behind a Cloudflare check), which holds the 80 percent and 1,000 MW paths by year and its renewable definition.
3. The start capacity of the 1,000 MW target (2022 or 2030), to compare with the model's installed renewable capacity (6,819 MW in NDCNEW 2035, against 4,354 MW in 2030).
4. The ENDBE update results (CLG OSeMOSYS high, medium and low scenarios), published by MARN, to replace the 2018 ENDBE as the LTS source; the 2018 ENDBE prints few numbers and several do not agree with each other.
5. AMM generation statistics, to recompute the 2022 to 2024 renewable share as the NDC defines it and to set the model's history against it.
6. A source for the ethanol blend in the model: the Guatemala model has no ethanol fuel, so the E10 target (GUA-T014) is a different quantity.
