# Notes for the country agents: shared sector data (GEN-201 to GEN-207)

All money values carry their own currency year; the `*_2024usd` columns use `cpi_u.csv` (GEN-001). FAOSTAT data are national data published by FAO: cite GEN-201 (trade), GEN-202 (producer prices) or GEN-203 (production, yields), bulk files retrieved 2026-11-22.

## Files
- `faostat_trade_unit_values.csv`: one row per country, model item and year 2019 to 2023, plus a row `avg_2019_2023`. Unit value = import value x 1000 / import quantity. The average row is quantity weighted in real 2024 USD (sum of deflated values / sum of quantities); the note gives the simple mean too and flags small volumes. Model unit MUSD/Mt equals US$ per t, so the value goes in as is (Honduras M US$ per kt: also US$ per t).
- `faostat_producer_prices.csv`: Producer Price (USD/tonne). Group items (vegetables, fruit, cereals, other grains, pulses, tubers, spices) are production-weighted means of the FAO items with a price that year (weights from GEN-203); cite GEN-202 and GEN-203. Guatemala has no FAO producer prices.
- `faostat_yields.csv`: production / area harvested, t per ha, group items summed. US$ per ha = US$ per t x t per ha (MUSD/Mha equals US$ per ha).
- `faostat_item_mapping.csv`: model item, the model techs it serves, the FAO item codes and labels. Choices to know: rice uses FAO 30 (rice milled equivalent); sugar uses raw plus refined sugar (162+164), with sugar cane (156) as an alternative since cane itself is hardly traded (tiny volumes, unreliable unit values); milk uses FAO 1881 (dairy products, milk equivalent); beef 1806 (carcass weight); pig and poultry meat FAO aggregates 2073 and 1808; "other grains" = cereals other than rice; Ecuador live animals (IMPBOV, IMPOVE, IMPCBR, IMPCAM, IMPCAB, IMPMUL, IMPPOR, IMPAVE) have both a live-animal row (US$ per head) and a meat row (US$ per t): pick the one that matches the model's activity unit. IMPPAL/AG_PAL (Palmito) is ambiguous: palm hearts (FAO 472, a broad preserved-vegetables item) or oil palm (FAO 257 palm oil, 254 oil palm fruit). DR IMPOTP "Other products" has no FAO match (row 1882 is an indicator only). Honduras "LEG" may mean vegetables rather than pulses; both are given.
- `gen_sector_values.csv`: waste and wastewater cost values, and one empty row per waste externality.

## Waste costs (model total per t = capital + variable)
- What a Waste 2.0, Table 5.2 (GEN-204, 2018 USD, factor 1.249): upper-middle income (CR, DR, ECU, GUA, JAM): collection and transfer 50 to 100 US$/t (62 to 125 real 2024), controlled to sanitary landfill 20 to 65 (25 to 81), composting 20 to 75 (25 to 94), open dumping not given. Lower-middle income (HON): collection 30 to 75, landfill 15 to 40, open dumping 3 to 10 (3.7 to 12.5 real 2024), composting 10 to 40. Totals only; no capital/operating split.
- CEPAL 2016, Cuadro 6 (GEN-206, LAC averages from the 2010 regional evaluation, 2010 USD, factor 1.439): collection 34.22, transfer 12.01, final disposal 20.43 US$/t (49.2, 17.3, 29.4 real 2024); collection, transfer and treatment together about 67 US$/t.
- Against the models: collection 9 + 29.7 = 38.7 sits below both sources once transfer is included; landfill 5 + 62.3 = 67.3 is near the top of the upper-middle range and above CEPAL; open dump 42.1 is three to four times the top of the only published range (3 to 10, lower-middle income). The split between CapitalCost and VariableCost is not given by any source; keep the model's split ratio if you scale.

## Wastewater (GEN-207, Hutton and Varughese 2016, Table E.1, 2015 USD, factor 1.323)
Capital per person served, urban: sewerage with treatment DR 447.2, ECU 660.4, GUA 384.9, JAM 339.9; septic tank with faecal sludge management DR 115.9, ECU 247.2, GUA 94.7, JAM 88.1. The model unit (M US$ per billion m3 per year) needs a volume per person: the rows divide by 54.75 m3 per person per year (150 L per day, ASSUMPTION, range 100 to 200 L). Converted, sewerage plus treatment is roughly 6,000 to 12,000 and septic 1,600 to 4,500 M US$ per billion m3 (2015 USD), against model values 1,194 (sewer) + 900 (aerobic plant) and 210 to 215 (septic). The sewerage figure covers conveyance and treatment together; split it only with a stated assumption. Costa Rica and Honduras rows of the table have empty cells and were not read; use the regional pattern or an ASSUMPTION.

## Task 3: origin of the waste externality penalties
The model documentation names Inter-American Development Bank publications as the origin: Correal, Rihm et al. (2023) "Lineamientos sectoriales para la gestion de residuos solidos y el avance hacia la economia circular" for health, water contamination and tourism costs; Correal and Rihm (2022) and an IDB study of the Mancomunidad Nororiente (Guatemala, 2023) for compost and recovered-material revenue; De la Pena et al. (2022, IDB) for reused water. All IDB publication pages and PDFs return a bot-verification page to scripts and to the browser pane, so none was opened and no value is confirmed: the penalties stay unsourced (`manual`, see `manual_downloads_GEN_sector.csv`). In every country the three penalties keep fixed ratios (salud = 2.43 x contam_agua, turismo = 3.64 x contam_agua) and decline over time, which points to one total per tonne split by shares and scaled by a time index. Honduras values are 1/1000 of the others because its unit is M US$ per kt.

## Caveats
FAO flags are kept in the trade file. Unit values on small volumes (for example Jamaica bananas, cane sugar imports, Guatemala soy) are unreliable. Jamaica maize and cereals producer prices (about 7,000 US$/t) look like an FAO data issue; check before use.
