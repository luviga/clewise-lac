## 1. Judgement calls

### R1 (formula cells)

1. **Guatemala `DIST_FLEXFUEL` VariableCost.** No Guatemalan or Central American price of a flex-fuel blend is published (MEM prints only tax-inclusive retail prices; the E10 portal prints no price). The region's flex-fuel market is Brazil, where flex-fuel cars burn hydrated ethanol (E100). The value is the published CEPEA/ESALQ weekly hydrated-ethanol indicator in US dollars (ex-mill, without freight, ICMS and PIS/Cofins, so pre-tax), annual mean, divided by 21.339 MJ/L (ANP: 0.809 t/m3 and 6,300 kcal/kg), converted to real 2024 USD with CPI-U for 2018 to 2024, and moved with the model's gasoline import-parity path (`DIST_GSL`, WEO 2025 STEPS) from 2025. It carries an ASSUMPTION sentence with the range 1.14 to 1.72 times pre-tax gasoline per GJ (Brazil, ANP and CEPEA, 2018 to 2024). Taking the published price rather than a flex-fuel to gasoline ratio follows the brief's wording ("the published price of the flex-fuel blend"). The row's mode went from "Percent growth of incomplete years" to "User defined" because the values now vary by year. Result: 26.94 USD/GJ (2018), 20.80 (2024), 20.58 (2050), against 45.31 to 71.89 before.
2. **Honduras `PPBGS` CapitalCost 2018 to 2020.** No national (PIEG 2020-2029 to 2026-2035 and annexes) or regional (Guatemala PEIG 2026-2050, Costa Rica ICE PEG 2024-2040, Panama ETESA 2023-2037) biogas capital cost exists. IRENA's Renewable Power Generation Costs editions checked (2014, 2022, 2025) give no biogas number, so the value comes from IRENA's predecessor series, Renewable Energy Technologies: Cost Analysis Series, Biomass for Power Generation (2012), Table 6.1 Case 10 (1 MW digester with engine on manure), 5,053 USD/kW in real 2010 USD, which is 7,269.10 USD/kW in 2024 USD. That is the same number the old formula carried (5053) without a currency conversion. "Consistent with the 2021 onward row" was read as same plant type, units and real 2024 USD; the 2021 onward row (5,390.58 USD/kW, falling to 3,870.85) has no proposal and no source and is 26 percent lower, so the series steps down in 2021 (section 2).
3. **Dominican Republic `ELE_TRANS` CapitalCost 2018.** The three investment inputs and the 2035 peak (5.501 GW) are sourced to the ETED expansion plan (Tabla 137 p.334, summary p.21; peak p.18 and Tabla 41 p.138). The formula's 8,670 hours appears nowhere in the plan; it was replaced by 8,760 hours (a 365-day year) under an ASSUMPTION sentence. The plan states no currency year; by the same rule as R4 the costs were taken as 2019 USD (cover dated August 2020) and converted to 2024 USD: 2.697109 x 1.226992 = 3.309335 M USD per PJ/yr (2.7251 before). The cited URL now returns HTTP 404; the same title was downloaded from ETED's new transparency portal, and byte identity with the cited file could not be proven (the Wayback copy would not download). Two cells recalculated with it: `J233` and `J253` are `=J232*0.5%` (fixed O&M at 0.5 percent of capital cost), 0.01363 to 0.01655.

## 2. Known-limitation candidates

Found during the run and not fixed (scope frozen).

1. **Ecuador refinery throughput.** The refinery crude input lower limit (`PROCRU`, 327.6 to 346.5 PJ in 2021 and 2022, 329.2 PJ from 2023) is above what the BCE series implies (45.5 million bbl processed in 2024, 262.8 PJ; production minus exports 275 PJ). It makes the model import crude in 2021 and 2022 (item 31) and from 2024.
2. **Ecuador crude after 2030.** Exports and extraction limits keep their current values from 2031 (no outlook found). In some of those years the kept export demand is above the kept extraction limit minus the refinery input, so the model imports crude at Brent to meet an export demand (the test solve imported 111 PJ in 2035). In later years extraction above the export demand leaves through `T5CRUEXP` above the demand, since export and extraction values are equal.
3. **Honduras `PPBGS` CapitalCost from 2021.** R1 replaced 2018 to 2020 with the sourced 7,269 USD/kW; the 2021-onward part of the row (5,390.58, the old `PPBIM` path) is unsourced, so the path drops in 2021.
4. **Dominican Republic distributed generation.** `PPDHYD` has a capacity factor of 0.0152 and `PPDBGS` 0.0812, so BAU's history activity lower limits make BAU build about 0.48 GW of distributed hydro (item 33).
5. **Costa Rica 2021 lower limits.** BAU's 2021 lower limits of `PWRHYDROR` and `PWRBMS` need slightly more capacity than the residual (item 35).
6. **Transport history differs between scenarios** (item 37), including electric-vehicle electricity demand in 2023 and 2024.
7. **B1 scripts differ between countries.** Costa Rica and Honduras do not apply the Security_Multiplier on Overwrite rows; the Dominican Republic and Ecuador round Exact emission-factor rows to 4 decimals; the others do not (items 32 and 38).
8. **Costa Rica duplicate config rows** for `PWRHYDROR` lower limits (rows 52 and 55, 61 and 64); B1 reads only the first.
9. **Jamaica run-of-river fleet.** The existing run-of-river plants sit in the `PPHDAM` residual (29.1 MW) while the Stage 2 history shares name `PPHROR` (item 28 moved only 2025 and 2026).
10. **Ecuador production sources.** EP Petroecuador's Plan Estrategico falls while the BCE outlook rises; only BCE was used.
11. **Dominican Republic `ELE_TRANS`.** The Spanish reference text in the workbook still describes the old method, and the identity of the ETED file behind the 467.89 MUSD figure could not be confirmed (its URL now returns 404).
12. **Guatemala flex fuel.** `DIST_FLEXFUEL` is valued at the Brazilian hydrated-ethanol price (ASSUMPTION); no Central American price series was found.
13. **Jamaica IRP-2.** Tables ES-4, ES-6, 11-2 and 11-5 give different totals and years for the small-hydro additions (item 26).
14. **PEIG 2026-2050 and PEN 2022-2036 currency years** are ASSUMPTIONS (year before publication).

## 5. Incidents

1. **First recompile.** Nine B1 solves were infeasible and two Jamaica data files unreadable, all caused by this job's writes; the Jamaica B1 run waited without end and was stopped (its own processes only). Causes, corrections and test solves: items 31 to 38 and `logs/phaseC_recompile_run1.log` to `run5.log`.
2. **Proposals rebuild.** A full rebuild of `proposals.csv` from the Phase A scripts read workbooks already written in Phase B and would have applied the Jamaica Stage 2 change twice; it was discarded and the committed file was patched row by row instead.
3. **A2 driver restores `A1_Outputs`** with `git checkout` after each compile, so a workbook changed after the last commit has to be staged before a recompile (the Ecuador A-O correction was staged).
4. **Excel.** EXCEL.EXE count was 0 at the start. The safe-save recalculation left an Excel process running for a few seconds after some saves; each time it ended on its own and the count was back to 0 before the next step. No Excel process was closed by the job.
5. **Disk.** Free space fell to about 80 GB during the diagnostic solves; the job's own diagnostic solver outputs in the session scratch folder (about 6 GB) were deleted, leaving 85 GB.
6. **Tools.** Git Bash curl crashed (segmentation fault) on two downloads, which then succeeded with Windows curl. The R1 research subagent reported writing a temporary file under /tmp outside the job folder; it held no model data.
